# Professional Identity

How I see myself, my work, and my trajectory - distilled from personal coaching.
This is the inner truth that drives positioning, summaries, and interview presence.
Not for direct inclusion in resumes, but the foundation everything else is built on.

---

## Professional Identity

- I'm a translator between what gets built and what gets bought. I've been the engineer shipping the feature, the consultant framing the decision, and the PM owning the roadmap. The thread through all of it is making complex technical value legible to the person who has to say yes.
- My differentiator is technical credibility plus commercial instinct. I can whiteboard the architecture with a prospect's engineering lead and reframe the ROI for their CFO in the same meeting.
- I'm happiest closest to the customer. Product taught me to love the problem; now I want to own the relationship where the problem gets solved and paid for.

## Strengths

- **Technical translation:** I came up through engineering, so I earn trust with technical buyers fast. I don't fake depth, I have it, and I can turn it into a business case.
- **Structured discovery:** BCG drilled the habit of finding the real problem before pitching a solution. In a sales motion that's qualification done right. I disqualify fast and pursue what's real.
- **Customer empathy from product:** Three years owning a roadmap means I instinctively map a buyer's stated ask to the underlying job they're trying to get done.

## Growth Edges

- **Carrying a number:** I've influenced revenue, never owned a quota. I'm deliberately moving toward the seat where the outcome is unambiguous and mine.
- **Patience with the long close:** Product ships in sprints; enterprise deals close in quarters. I'm building the discipline to nurture a six-month cycle without forcing it.
- **Pitching before fully understanding:** My product reflex is to jump to the solution. In sales discovery I'm training myself to stay in the question longer.

## Narrative Patterns

| Default framing | Reframe |
|---|---|
| "I was a PM on the platform team" | "I owned the roadmap for a product line that grew to $8M ARR, working hand-in-hand with the AEs who sold it" |
| "I helped sales with technical questions" | "I ran the technical evaluation for our top 10 enterprise deals as the product partner in the room" |
| "I used to be an engineer" | "I shipped production code for three years, so I close technical buyers other sellers can't" |

## Work Style & Values

- **Customer-facing energy:** I gain energy from live conversations and lose it in long solo stretches. That's different from my engineering days, and it's a big part of why this pivot fits.
- **Direct communication:** I'd rather hear a fast no than a slow maybe, and I give the same in return.
- **Coachable:** I'm new to the sales craft and I want a manager who will run deals with me, not just inspect the pipeline.

## Career Direction

- Moving from product management into enterprise sales or sales engineering at a technical B2B SaaS company
- Want a team where technical selling is valued, not where I'm handed a script
- Drawn to products I'd have wanted to buy as a PM: developer tools, data infrastructure, AI-native workflow software
- Targeting a quota-carrying AE or Sales Engineer seat with a clear path to owning larger enterprise accounts

## Core Values (Hierarchy)

1. **Mastery** - getting genuinely good at a hard craft (the close)
2. **Customer impact** - selling something I believe actually helps
3. **Earnings upside** - I want my outcomes tied to my performance
4. **Team** - selling alongside people I learn from
5. **Autonomy** - own my territory and my calendar
