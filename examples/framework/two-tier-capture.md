# Two-Tier Capture

## The principle

Keep **raw** captured material and **synthesized** artifacts in separate tiers. Never let synthesis overwrite the raw source, and never let the raw dump masquerade as a finished artifact.

- **Tier 1 — Raw (frozen):** what was actually said or written, verbatim. Interview transcripts, your own dictated reflections, sent emails, meeting notes. Append-only; never edited for polish. This is the ground truth everything else is checked against.
- **Tier 2 — Synthesized (living):** the distilled artifact — a coached answer, a follow-up draft, a CV bullet, a cross-session theme. Always traceable back to a Tier-1 source.

## Why it matters

- **Voice fidelity:** drafts generated from your real raw words sound like you; drafts generated from prior synthesis drift toward generic.
- **Truth-checking:** when a synthesized claim looks off, you can diff it against the frozen raw source instead of trusting memory.
- **No silent rewrites:** freezing the raw tier prevents a polishing pass from quietly erasing what really happened.

## In practice

- A debrief reads the raw transcript (Tier 1) and produces refined coached answers (Tier 2) — the transcript is never edited.
- A follow-up sources its specific callback from the raw call transcript (Tier 1, content only) while drawing voice from your sent-email corpus — it doesn't invent.
- Reflections you dictate are frozen per-session (Tier 1); the cross-session synthesis lives in a separate living file (Tier 2).

**Rule of thumb:** if a file is something you *wrote/said*, freeze it. If it's something the system *distilled*, keep it living and traceable.
