## The discipline

When you dictate raw thinking (walking, driving, between meetings), the captured text is **yours** — preserve it as voice-pure and keep the system's synthesis separate from it.

## Rules

- **Capture verbatim first.** Land the raw dictation in a frozen, append-only file before any cleanup. The dump *is* the thinking, not a rough draft of it.
- **Minimal-polish only.** Fix obvious transcription errors and punctuation; do not rewrite phrasing, restructure, or "improve" the prose. Light touch.
- **Never author in your voice.** The system may add its own synthesis (themes, prompts, a reframe) — but in a clearly separate section labeled as the system's read, never inside the voice-pure block. Your voice and the system's voice never blend on the page.
- **An empty reaction is a signal, not a gap to fill.** If a captured item has no take from you yet, leave it empty — that's a backlog marker, not an invitation for the system to write one for you.
- **Date by the session, not the clock.** A reflection belongs to the day/session it's about, even if you transcribe it later.

## Why

The point of dictation is to reach depth you can't reach by typing, in your own register. The moment synthesis overwrites the raw voice, you lose the one thing that made the capture worth keeping — and future drafts that read from it inherit generic phrasing instead of yours.
