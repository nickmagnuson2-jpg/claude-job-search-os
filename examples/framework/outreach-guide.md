## Message frameworks (pick by context)

- **Persona-Based** — lead with a credential or shared context the recipient recognizes; best for alumni / warm-adjacent contacts.
- **3Ps (Praise / Picture / Push)** — genuine specific praise, a picture of the opportunity, one clear ask.
- **PAS (Problem / Agitate / Solve)** — name a problem they have, sharpen it, position yourself as part of the solution.
- **BAB (Before / After / Bridge)** — their current state, a better state, you as the bridge. Good for value-led pitches.
- **AIDA (Attention / Interest / Desire / Action)** — classic top-to-bottom; use when you have a strong hook.

## Channel constraints

- Email: **75-125 words**. Subject lines 2-4 words; questions outperform statements.
- LinkedIn connection request: **≤ 300 characters**.
- LinkedIn InMail: **≤ 1,900 characters** (but shorter still wins).

## Personalization waterfall (most to least specific)

1. A specific thing they wrote, shipped, or said publicly (best).
2. A shared, named connection or affiliation.
3. A precise, current trigger (their funding round, a launch, a role they posted).
4. Generic "I admire the company" — last resort; usually means do more homework.

## The three-question quality gate (all three must be strong)

- **Why you?** — what you concretely bring.
- **Why now?** — the timely trigger that makes this the right moment.
- **Why me?** — the personalization that proves this isn't a blast.

Write a specific one-sentence answer to each before sending. If any is weak, the message isn't ready.

## Tone matching

Read 2-3 of your prior sent messages to this recipient (or your general sent corpus) and match your own natural register. Peer-to-peer, concise, no filler. If you have no prior thread, default to professional and direct.

## Follow-up cadence

- 3-5 touches at 3-5 day spacing.
- Each touch must add **new value** (an insight, an article, a shared connection, a fresh angle) — never "just checking in."
- Each subsequent message shorter than the last.

## Anti-patterns (don't)

- Filler openers ("I hope this finds you well", "I'm reaching out because", "I wanted to connect").
- Hedging ("I was just wondering if maybe", "not sure if this is right but").
- Essay structure (context > context > context > ask). Lead with the ask or the value.
- Volunteering negatives or apologizing for reaching out.
- Vague CTAs ("let me know your thoughts"). Specify what, when, and how.
